01 / Context

What I was trying to change

KitLabFS is an online store for club, national-team and retro football jerseys, with 1,200+ products and 7,822 variants. I designed and launched it, owning product pages, funnels, checkout and inventory flows. The store drew 450,000+ visitors in its first three months and grew to 550+ orders.

02 / Problem

The interface was only the symptom.

Choices such as player versions, patches, sizing and name customization were hard to understand. Shoppers also dropped off between the cart and the completed purchase.

The questionHow might we help customers move from thousands of jerseys to a confident purchase, without making any step feel overloaded?

05 / Solution

The design bet

Sequence the journey instead of exposing every choice at once.
Compare

Mobile and detail

Drag the handle to compare the first product page with the redesign.

BeforeAfter
What we saw

Shoppers treated the page as a toy. Every switch revealed a price and a box to type in, so people tried their own name, watched the price change and left. Those sessions inflated product views and checkout visits with no intent to buy.

What changed

We compacted the choices into one sequence: version, size, personalization and a visible price breakdown, with no switches to flip. The total is shown before the button.

Shipping up front

Delivery and free shipping now sit in the buy panel. Before, some customers reached checkout, found the shipping cost and left. Now they decide before they get there.

Why it matters

Less playing around means cleaner funnel data. Shoppers who reach checkout have already made the decision, so they finish faster and with fewer second thoughts.

01 / Context

What I was trying to change

MatchUp is a mobile app for creating local soccer matches, selecting available positions and sharing field-booking costs. It connects organizers who have booked a field with players looking for a local game.

02 / Problem

The interface was only the symptom.

Players rely on scattered group chats with incomplete information about cost, skill level and open positions. Organizers manually build teams and repeatedly chase payments.

The questionHow might we create one trusted experience for forming teams, selecting positions and sharing field costs, for two different users at once?

INOXAR

Give a stainless-steel maker a mark that looks as precise as the work it builds.

RoleLogo and brand identity design
ClientINOXAR, stainless-steel kitchens and architecture
TimelineSep 2026
ScopeLogo, versions, palette, usage rules and applications

The 30-second version

The problem

INOXAR designs industrial kitchens, architecture and stainless-steel solutions.

The design bet

Build the symbol from four separate steel pieces that lock into one system.

The outcomeVersions

Colour and one-ink versions for light and dark backgrounds.

01 / Context

A brand built like the product.

INOXAR works in stainless steel, so the identity starts from the material: modular pieces, clean edges and exact alignment. I designed the logo, its versions and the rules for using it, then delivered them as a Logo Book so the brand stays consistent across documents, signage, products and ads.

INOXAR designs industrial kitchens, architecture and stainless-steel solutions. Its mark had to feel technical and trustworthy, work on an engraved plate as well as on a quote or a website, and stay consistent in anyone’s hands.

02 / The idea

Four pieces, one block.

Build the symbol from four separate steel pieces that lock into one system. Each piece is a module cut from stainless steel, so the symbol reads as something fabricated and fitted together.

Hover or tap a step to replay it. Drawn in code from the real logo geometry: each module starts as a plain steel slab and is cut down to its final shape.

Modularity

Four independent pieces that work as one system.

Precision

Controlled geometry, alignment and a clear read at any size.

Stainless steel

A direct reference to the material, its strength and how it is fabricated.

Architecture and kitchens

Made for real projects, furniture and technical solutions.

03 / The system

One mark, three jobs.

01

Full logo

Symbol, name and descriptor. For documents, quotes, presentations, the web and main communication.

02

INOXAR name

The wordmark alone, for horizontal spaces where the name should lead.

03

Symbol

For plates, avatars, icons and small spaces.

Turquoise#08A9E6
Steel gray#B8BFC4
Carbon#1E2430
White#FFFFFF

04 / The rules

So it stays consistent.

The descriptor travels with the full logo in documents, presentations, the web and commercial pieces. Drop it when the logo is under 50 mm wide, and on icons, avatars, small engravings and compact plates.

Also out: gradients, outlines, new spacing and splitting the pieces apart.

05 / In use

On a plate, a wall, a card and a street.

Applications are concept mockups that show how the system holds up in context.

06 / What I delivered

A system, not just a logo.

4

Versions

Colour and one-ink versions for light and dark backgrounds.

3

Lockups

Full logo, name only and symbol, each with a job.

1

Rulebook

A Logo Book with sizes, spacing, do and don’t.

The wider identity (typography, graphic system, stationery) is the next phase.
